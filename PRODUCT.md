# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two people at Quminex open this from a link in a Riipen application or an email, on a laptop, in office hours, with an ARIS PDF a tab away.

- Alan Baxter, co-founder and Chief Exploration Officer, a P.Geo. He knows Iron Lake's kind of ground and BC assessment reports. He is skeptical of AI in exploration and trusts a value only when he can see the page it came from. He will click a number, look at the page, and decide in ten minutes whether the builder can lead track 1 of their Level UP project (an agent that turns technical reports into a cited property evaluation).
- Marc Fassbender, CEO, an economic geologist who has said ML in geoscience is easy to misuse. He wants to see an agent system working on real data: what it did, how long it took, what it cost, and where it failed.

Their technical people, and anyone they forward the link to, will look closer at the machinery.

## Product Purpose

A Claude agent read seven public BC assessment reports on Iron Lake (566 pages) in 27 parallel sessions and built a property file. Code checked every claim it proposed against the cited page, inside the tool call, and the agent retried when it was told no. Where reports disagree, code read the reports' own drill collar tables and lab certificates to say which side they back. A second session wrote a cited summary from the file, and 15 questions written before the run scored the agent.

The site is that recorded run, replayable, and the file it produced. Success: Alan replays the run, clicks three values, finds each on its page, and sees the 62 km location error and the IL-22-20 lab mismatch caught without being told where to look. Marc sees 27 sessions finish in 7.5 minutes for about $12, with every rejection and miss shown.

## Brand Personality

Exact, plain, quietly confident. A good internal tool at an exploration-data company: numbers first, short labels, nothing to sell. It borrows Quminex's own look (dark slate bands, their electric blue, the copper glow in their imagery, Plus Jakarta Sans) so it reads as something their team could run.

## Anti-references

- The generic AI demo: centered marketing hero, gradient blobs, glass cards, emoji, badge soup, "Powered by AI" chips.
- The essay page: beige serif, paragraphs before product, numbered "Try this first" walkthroughs.
- The hero-metric row: four big-number cards with small labels.
- Agent theatre: typing effects, "thinking" spinners, invented progress, any step or time the logs don't hold.
- Walls of small grey text.

## Design Principles

1. The product is the first screen. The run and the file it made, with no introduction. Method lives behind one "How it works" click.
2. Every mark is traceable. Each tick, value, point and score opens its tool call, its page, its rule or its lab row.
3. Say who did what. Claude's work and code's work are labelled apart, and code is never dressed up as AI.
4. The viewer drives. Replay the run, scrub it, pick a session, open a value, pick a drill hole, filter to the rejections.
5. Keep the caveat, cut the words. A number keeps the qualifier it needs to stay true, in as few words as that takes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. State is never color alone: each has a word, a glyph or a shape. Every chart mark is reachable by keyboard or has a keyboard path to the same data. Replay starts only when asked, and reduced motion skips eased transitions. Works at 375 px wide with no sideways scroll.
